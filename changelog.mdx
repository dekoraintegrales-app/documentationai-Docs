## Release workflow

To keep your changelog useful, follow a simple release workflow. First, write down the change while it is still fresh. Then review the entry for clarity, and finally publish it with a version label and date.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft the update" icon="edit">
    
Write a short summary of what changed, who it affects, and why it matters.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Group the notes" icon="list">
    
Separate the entry into sections such as New Features, Improvements, and Bug Fixes.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish the release" icon="rocket">
    
Add the final date and version number, then publish the update for your readers.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Publishing examples

## Documentation areas to review

Use the checklist below when you revise a release. It helps you keep the changelog aligned with the rest of your documentation.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Content" icon="book-open">
    
Check page copy, headings, and section order. Make sure each entry uses the same style and tone.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Structure" icon="layout-grid">
    
Verify that each update has a date, a version label, and at least one meaningful note.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Quality" icon="shield">
    
Review links, formatting, and terminology before you publish the page.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Best practices

Keep each note short enough to scan in a few seconds. Use active voice, avoid vague phrasing, and describe the actual outcome of the change. If you add a new page, mention what it covers and why it helps readers.
