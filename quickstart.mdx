## Quickstart overview

Welcome to Diseño Dekora. This starter kit shows you how to move from first contact to a custom furniture project without needing to know every detail up front.

Use this page as a practical starting point: review the catalog, choose a project type, share your measurements, and prepare for your first consultation.

<Callout kind="info">
  This is sample documentation you can adapt to your own workflow, service areas, and response times.
</Callout>

## Follow the first-contact workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request a consultation" icon="message-circle">
    Ask for the best way to continue, such as a form submission, a phone call, or a visit request. Keep the first message short and clear.

    Mention the room type, the approximate size of the space, and the general style you want.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the catalog" icon="package">
    Compare the main categories before you make a decision. A typical starting point is to review kitchens, closets, bathroom furniture, and workspaces.

    If you are building documentation for your own brand, replace these categories with the exact product groups you offer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a project type" icon="layout-grid">
    Decide whether you need a single piece, a room-specific design, or a broader home solution.

    A clear project type helps the team estimate materials, scope, and lead time more accurately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share measurements and preferences" icon="clipboard-list">
    Send dimensions, room photos, preferred materials, and any functional requirements.

    Include constraints such as doors, windows, outlets, or appliances so the proposal matches the real space.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm next steps" icon="arrow-right">
    After the first contact, confirm what happens next: a site review, a concept proposal, a quotation, or a design revision.

    Keep the process visible so the customer knows when to expect a response.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Compare common project paths

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Kitchen" icon="utensils">
    
    A kitchen project usually starts with cooking habits, storage needs, and appliance sizes. You should ask about workflow, preferred finishes, and whether the layout must include an island or breakfast bar.

    Good inputs include wall measurements, sink placement, and the number of users in the space.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Closet" icon="shirt">
    
    A closet project focuses on clothing volume, shoe storage, and accessory organization. You can also ask whether the customer wants open shelving, closed doors, or a mixed solution.

    Measurements should include ceiling height, available wall width, and any fixed elements that limit installation.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Bathroom" icon="droplets">
    
    A bathroom furniture project typically balances compact storage with moisture-resistant materials. Ask for plumbing locations, mirror preferences, and vanity dimensions.

    This is the best place to confirm sink size, drawer needs, and cleaning considerations.

  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Workspace" icon="monitor">
    
    A workspace project should support focus, cable management, and everyday use. Gather laptop or desktop dimensions, equipment list, and the amount of surface area needed.

    If the space is shared, note how many people will use it and whether the layout must support meetings or storage.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## What to do after first contact

1. Confirm the project category and the room to be designed.
2. Share measurements, photos, and any special constraints.
3. Wait for a response with questions, an estimate, or a proposal.
4. Review the suggested solution and request adjustments if needed.
5. Approve the next stage, such as a visit, a design draft, or a quote.

<Expandable title="What should you prepare before contacting the team?" default-open="true">
  
  Prepare the room measurements, a short description of your goals, and any images that show the existing space. If you already know your preferred style or color palette, include that too.

  The more specific you are, the easier it is to align the first proposal with your expectations.

</Expandable>

<Expandable title="What if you only have a rough idea?" default-open="false">
  
  That is enough to begin. You can start with a general category, such as kitchen or closet, and refine the details after the first conversation.

  This approach works well when you want guidance on materials, layout, or storage needs.

</Expandable>

<Callout kind="tip">
  Keep your first request simple, specific, and visual. A short message with measurements and a few reference notes usually gets the fastest response.
</Callout>
